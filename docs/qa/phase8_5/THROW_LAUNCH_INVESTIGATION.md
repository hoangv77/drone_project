# Throw Launch recovery investigation

QA-01, QA-06, QA-08 and QA-15. The preserved audit recorded two orientation failures with native detection, armed FAULT, and near-ground pose 0.692 simulated seconds after release. The companion waited for its roughly 15-second stabilization deadline before attempting recovery. The pinned firmware releases native lockdown after its velocity conditions and lacks an attitude veto in that path. The companion's tilt threshold is a post-release completion predicate. It cannot prevent that release.

Confirmed LAND mode transitions show the controller still responded to commands. They do not prove motor authority, attitude recovery, landing or disarm. In the orientation trials the near-ground timing preceded the companion's recovery deadline. In the below-threshold trial native release was not observed and the final flags were armed and landed. Three LAND confirmations exhausted the companion's bounded attempts. The logs do not establish that LAND was dynamically achievable under the simulated conditions. The aircraft model has provisional mass, thrust, inertia, contact and sensor parameters. It cannot establish a physical recovery envelope.

The SITL verdict now independently requires execution, scenario behavior and fresh terminal evidence. An armed FAULT fails. A nominal trial establishes only stabilized airborne hold with independent short-window pose evidence. For negative trials the checker requires a fresh landed/disarmed terminal condition and no prohibited pose-height contact proxy. Trial classifications do not modify the simulator or flight controller.

The pending-service-response and ambiguous-arming reconciliation problem remains open. Changing when the coordinator issues LAND or DISARM, clearing uncertain arming state, and introducing an orientation veto are separate safety-case decisions. Require independent review and validation before implementing any physical release or recovery policy. Physical Throw Launch remains prohibited.
